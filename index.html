Void Tarot

A tarot deck that behaves like a real one

A full 78-card deck built around the cards themselves — you drag the deck, fan it, and turn cards with your thumb, with the physics and haptics of the real thing. Five spreads are free; a one-time purchase unlocks six more.

  • Full 78-card deck with reviewed upright and reversed meanings
  • Tactile deck — direct-drag fan, velocity-aware release, 3D flips with haptics and sound
  • Interpretation generated on your device; readings aren't sent to a server
  • Private journal and reading history, synced only through your own iCloud
  • No account, no email, no sign-in gate

Simorgh

Nomads and land-based community projects, matched

A marketplace connecting traveling nomads with intentional communities that have housing capacity and work that needs hands. We spent most of the design time on trust and safety, because that's the part that decides whether any of it works.

  • Discover communities and project calls by map or list
  • Hosts post stays with real availability, capacity, and stay rules
  • Requests, acceptances, and messages are all enforced server-side
  • Reciprocal reviews that unlock after a stay is completed
  • Private reporting, directional blocking, and a moderation queue
  • Opt-in safety help — approximate location only, one specific audience, two-hour expiry
V1 in build iOS & Android · Expo · React Native

How we build

How the two apps are put together.

Native Swift Void Tarot is Swift and SwiftUI on iOS 26. The card handling needs direct access to gestures and haptics, so it's written straight against the platform — 120 FPS on ProMotion.
On-device generation Readings are produced locally with Apple's Foundation Models, so your questions and journal entries stay on your phone.
CloudKit sync Void Tarot syncs through your own private CloudKit database. We don't hold a copy and can't read it.
Written down first Every behavior change updates the spec in the same commit and ships against an automated test matrix. It's slow, but it's how we like to work.

Where the name comes from. Sī murgh is Persian for "thirty birds" — the pun at the heart of a 12th-century poem in which thirty birds travel a long way to find a legendary bird, and turn out to be it. It seemed like a reasonable name for a product about people finding each other.
